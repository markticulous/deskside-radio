Deskside Radio    v1.0.0

Browsers need one click before audio can play, so opening the page the ordinary way gets the usual tap panel, already tuned to this station. Run Create Desktop Shortcut.cmd once and the shortcut it makes opens the radio in a window that starts on its own.

Pick a city, then search by name or frequency.

    Listings come from radio-browser.info; the city lookup uses open-meteo. Direct MP3 and AAC streams play here. Entries flagged as HLS can still be added, but may not play.

    End time is exclusive: 07:00 to 10:00 starts at 07:00:00 and stops at 10:00:00. A slot may run past midnight. Between slots the current station keeps playing. Whatever a slot applies wins over the settings in use at the time.

    Backup

    A settings file holds the stations, the schedule, the theme and the sound settings. Nothing else leaves this machine.

    Updates

    Once a day the app asks GitHub whether a newer version has been published. It sends nothing but the request itself — no settings, no listening history — and never installs anything on its own.

    Reset

    Puts everything back to a fresh install: the three original stations, an empty schedule, no remembered city, and the sound settings at their middle.

    No frequency set

    Without one the dial has nothing to point at and the AM or FM badge stays dark. Enter it as 99.1 FM or 1010 AM. Internet-only stations genuinely have no frequency, and saving without one is fine.

    Reset to defaults?

    Every station you have added goes, along with the whole schedule, the remembered city, and your volume and tone settings. NewsTalk 1010 and KISS 92.5 come back. This cannot be undone.

    Shortcut downloaded

    Your browser will not save a shortcut file under its own name — it renames it, because a shortcut can point anywhere. Two ways to finish:

    1. Rename it. In your Downloads folder, rename Deskside Radio.download to Deskside Radio.url, then drag it to your Desktop.
