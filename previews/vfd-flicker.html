Deskside Radio · Broadcast console

How the phosphor should misbehave

Five readings of “a subtle random flicker at random intervals”, on the real VFD Nova face with the theme’s own four-layer phosphor stack behind it. They are running now. Watch for thirty seconds before choosing — the whole question is whether a thing you only catch out of the corner of your eye reads as a display or as a fault.

—
A Filament drift No events at all. The emission wanders a few percent, the way a warm cathode does. Nothing ever “happens”.

CBC RADIO ONE

11.3s cycle, unround on purpose. Cheapest of the five and the least likely to be noticed at all — which may be the point, or may be too little.

B Driver stutter Held dead steady, then a 60–90ms dip, at uneven spacing. Sometimes two in quick succession.

CBC RADIO ONE

8.7s cycle, stepped. The steadiness between dips is what makes a dip read as a fault rather than as a pulse.

C Weak grid Two glyphs misbehave and the rest do not. This is how a real VFD fails: per character, because each one is its own grid.

CBC RADIO ONE

Needs the text split into spans. Most faithful to the object; also the only one that changes the markup the readout is built from.

D Surge bloom The glow briefly widens instead of the level dropping — a moment of over-drive rather than a brown-out.

CBC RADIO ONE

Spread ×1.35–1.5 for ~150ms. Reads warmer and less broken than a dip. Against the brief: this is a glow that grows, and you asked for a small spread.

E Genuinely random Script picks the next event 3–14s out and dips for 40–110ms, sometimes twice. No cycle to find.

CBC RADIO ONE

One timer, no animation frame loop. The only one that never repeats — a keyframe cycle does, and the eye is good at finding the repeat.

What it costs

A, B and D repaint the text every frame they are changing, because text-shadow is a paint property — there is no compositor shortcut for a glow. That is fine for a dip lasting 90ms and not fine for something that runs continuously, which is why B and E are cheaper in practice than A: they are still between events almost all of the time.

C is the only one that needs the readout re-built as one span per character. The marquee already wraps the line in a runner when a name is too long to fit, so there is a precedent, but it is more machinery than the other four.

E costs one timer and is idle between events. It is the only option where the interval is genuinely random rather than a cycle dressed up as one.
