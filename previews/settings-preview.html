Three ways to lay out Settings

The same six stations and three time slots in each, so the comparison is about structure rather than content. Tabs, sections and station rows all work, so click around.

All three carry the same four changes listed below. Those are what actually fixes the clutter; the lanes only differ in how the four areas are arranged.

Decided: A, the tab strip

This page is the record of the choice, not the finished thing. Option A is now built into the app, along with everything in the shared list below. Two details changed on the way in: the tabs lost their folder borders for a single accent bar that slides between them, and the thumbnails now show a made-up station, CDSK on 68.0, so none of them reads as a real broadcaster.

The app also gained three things this page never showed: bass and treble are remembered per station, a schedule slot can impose volume, bass, treble and theme with a switch for each, and double-clicking any fader returns it to its resting position.

Shared by all three

  • One line per station. Colour rail, name, frequency pill, actions. Click a row to expand the full form. Six stations is six lines instead of eighteen form rows.
  • Search folds away. The city and station lookup sits behind one bar until you want it.
  • Cards, not rows. Each station carries a rail in its own colour so the list reads as separate things.
  • Play on launch. A switch and a station picker at the top of Stations. Off by default; the browser still needs one click before audio starts, so when it blocks the start you get the usual tap panel and that station is what it tunes.
  • Theme thumbnails. Four drawn miniatures rather than four words. Nothing to load and they cannot go stale.

Theme thumbnails

Drawn in CSS, so they cost nothing to load and cannot drift out of date. Each one is a miniature of its tuner: cabinet, display, band mark and preset keys. These appear in the Look area of every lane below.

Each mock sheet below is about 400 px wide so all three fit side by side. The real drawer is wider, so where a card here wraps its buttons onto a second line, in the app it stays on one.
