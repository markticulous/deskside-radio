Eight faces for one radio

Every theme the app ships, drawn here by the app's own stylesheet and the same instrument code, so this page cannot go out of date without the app going with it. Same controls in each: now-playing display, presets, play and stop, volume, scheduler state, settings. Only the visual language changes, and the presets and buttons are live, so click around.

Dial scales are drawn to the real broadcast bands, so the needle sits at 1010 kHz or 92.5 MHz where a receiver would put it. The meters here run on a simulated signal; in the app they read the actual stream, which is why the Model One speaker moves without a station being tuned.

Analogue dial is the default, and the rest are one click away in Settings, Theme. Byline options live on the byline page.

A

Vintage analogue dial

Current default

Walnut cabinet, brass hardware, backlit cream glass carrying both band scales. The lit red badge says which band you are on. Preset keys light up when selected and sit dark when not.

B

Broadcast console

Rack-mounted matte black on real mounting screws, with a readout built as a vacuum fluorescent display rather than amber type on black: an undriven dot field across the character band, grid wires on the glass, and a 14-segment face. The transport flashes while the source comes up and goes steady on air. Segmented LED meter, -20 to +3 VU. Nothing rounded.

C

Braun / Dieter Rams minimal

Off-white body, hairline rules, one orange marker on the single scale for whichever band is tuned. Small type, restrained weight, nothing decorative.

D

Editorial

Type does the work. Each station owns a colour and the block cross-fades on switch. The frequency always drops to its own line and the name grows to the largest size the panel allows, so it never wraps mid-word. A bank of level bars replaces the VU meter here, mirrored about a centre line and rising and falling in place.

E

Retro 8-bit

A game HUD. Four colours off the NES palette, everything on an 8px grid, no curves and no gradients — the only shading is a two-pixel dither, with scanlines over the whole face. The level meter quantises into cells instead of sweeping, and the presets are a menu with a cursor.

F

Departures board

Split-flap. The station name sits on a flap grid with its fold seam, the presets read as board rows, and the meter is built from the same flaps as the type. The one theme where the aesthetic does work the app already needs: the schedule becomes the subject.

G

Marconi deco

Black lacquer and gold with the sunburst every 1930s cabinet wore over its speaker, masked to a ring so the type stays readable on top of it. Cinzel over Poiret One, and the analogue VU returns in a gold palette — the same instrument, repainted.

H

Model One

The object rather than an interface about it: a cherry cabinet with the driver at one end, a cream plate across the middle and one machined knob at the other. Jost, which no other theme uses. There is no VU here — the speaker is the meter, and waves leave the dust cap and run out across the cloth as far as the level sends them.
