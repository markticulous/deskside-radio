Four faces for the time of year

Seasonal themes, drawn by the app's own stylesheet and instrument code, exactly as the other eight are. Each one is offered in Settings, Theme only during its month, with a badge saying when it goes. If one is still showing when its month ends, the radio quietly goes back to whatever theme it had before.

Each is built on a theme that already ships: Halloween and Harvest on the Analogue dial, Christmas on the dial's glass with the meter across the top, Spring on Rams. The meters run on a simulated signal here. Press stop on any of them to see it settle: the bats, leaves, snow and rain only move while the set is playing.

The fourth letter column reads the month. Halloween is the one to judge first, since October starts next week.

Oct

Halloween Fun

October only

A 1930s wireless that has been left in the attic too long. The glass is lit by a candle and flickers with it; a spider keeps the corner. The meter is a jack-o’-lantern: its face burns as bright as the programme is loud and spills a halo onto the glass. Presets are headstones, and the one playing has risen. While it plays, bats cross the cabinet, fog drifts along the floor, lightning cracks when it comes on air and now and then after, and something in the dark either side opens its eyes. The fader handles are candy corn.

Nov

Autumn Gobble

November only

A barn-red farmhouse set, board and batten with white trim. The panel is lamp-lit feed-store paper in Rye wood type. The meter is a window onto the field at dusk, and the moon over it rises and burns brighter as the level climbs. Geese go south across it, maple leaves come down over the whole set, and the presets are stencilled crate labels. Twine track, wooden pegs. Now and then a turkey runs along the bottom, and sometimes thinks better of it; very rarely, something else happens.

Dec

Noel

December only

A cranberry Bakelite cabinet with gold trim and holly on the corner. The window is a winter night under twinkling stars, one brighter than the rest, with snowflakes coming down and pines on the hills. The string of lights along the top is the meter: bulbs come on one whole bulb at a time, left to right, on the same cell grid the console uses. Presets are gift tags, and the chosen one is wrapped. The play button is a bauble, the handles are candy canes. Now and then a snowman builds himself, an elf changes a bulb, or a cardinal stops on the holly; very rarely, something else happens.

Apr

April Showers

April only

Rams’ restraint with the weather let in. The panel is a wet sky with rain running across it. The meter lies along the sill: a bed of tulips that comes up out of the grass as far as the level lifts it, and a puddle whose rain rings reach as far as the level sends them — the Model One speaker, laid flat. Fraunces italic over Figtree; buds on the presets flower when chosen.
