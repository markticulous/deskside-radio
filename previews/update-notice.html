Where an update should announce itself

The check already runs and already knows. What it has never had is anywhere to say so outside the Settings drawer. Five placements below, each drawn into the radio’s own top bar across four of the eight themes — whatever is chosen has to sit in one place and read the same way in all of them.

What I would pick

A, on its own

A dot on Settings is the only treatment that costs the radio nothing. It adds no object to a bar that eight themes each lay out differently, needs no colour of its own beyond the theme accent already in use, and points at the drawer where the version line and the releases link already live.

An update is not urgent. It is worth knowing about and worth ignoring for a fortnight, which is exactly what a dot says and a pill does not.

recommended

B, if it must be readable

Pick this if the dot would go unnoticed on your machine for months. It is the only option that names the thing, and the wordmark end of the bar has room in every theme.

Cost: a new object in the header that each theme has to be checked against, and one more thing competing with the schedule chip for attention.

Not D or E

The hairline collides with the countdown that already runs along the schedule chip in the last minute of a slot — two hairlines meaning different things is worse than no hairline.

The toast covers the station name, which is the one thing the radio exists to show.

How often

Every six hours

As asked. Four requests a day to one static file, still nothing sent but the request. A radio left open all week would ask about twenty-eight times instead of seven.

It only helps where the app stays open for days at a time — which is exactly what this app is for, so it is the right call.

What it changes

One constant: CHECK_EVERY from 24 hours to 6. The stored timestamp and the forced check behind Check now are untouched.

The readmes both say “once a day” in two places and would be corrected with it.

Mock bars are reduced drawings, not screenshots — brand, maker plate, clock, schedule chip, shortcut and Settings, with each theme’s own ground, ink and accent. The remaining four themes (editorial, departures, marconi, Model One) sit between the extremes shown here.
